# Candidate Outreach for Recruiting

## Purpose

Transform sourcing intelligence (personas, talent maps, JDs) into compelling, personalized outreach that gets responses. Cover the full outreach lifecycle: first touch, follow-ups, channel switches, breakup messages, and response handling.

## Outreach Principles

1. **Candidate-centric value prop** - Lead with what matters to THEM, not what the company needs
2. **Specific > generic** - Reference something concrete about their background, never "I came across your profile"
3. **Shorter wins** - InMail: under 400 characters (~50-70 words) gets 22% higher response. Email: 101-150 words is the sweet spot. Respect their time.
4. **One CTA** - Single, low-commitment ask. "15-min chat" not "apply now"
5. **Multi-channel compounds** - Combining channels increases engagement by up to 287% (LinkedIn data)
6. **Follow-up is where deals happen** - A 4-stage sequence gets 2x more replies and 68% higher interested rates vs one-off messages (Gem, 4M emails)
7. **Tone matches channel** - LinkedIn = professional, GitHub = developer-authentic, Twitter = casual
8. **Know when to stop** - 4 touches per sequence, then pause. Data flattens after stage 4-5.
9. **Sound Human** - Make it sound human, authentic and conversational. Don't be overly formal. Don't use staccato prose
10. **Leverage the hiring manager** - SOBO (Send On Behalf Of) improves reply rates by 50%+. Only 22% of recruiters use it. Send first touch from the hiring manager or team lead for leadership hires and key ICs.
11. **Measure what matters** - Track *interested* rate, not just reply rate. Gem data: 21.3% reply but only 7.9% interested — only 37% of replies are positive. Optimize for interested replies.
12. **Guard sensitive information** - Outreach is semi-public (candidates forward messages, screenshot them, post them). Never include: exact revenue/ARR, deal sizes/ACV, pricing, internal targets, headcount plans not yet announced, customer contract values, or strategic timelines (fundraising, M&A). Use the *signal* ("mid-market SaaS", "Series A stage", "growing fast") without the *number*.

## Message Anatomy

Every outreach message follows this structure (adapt length per channel):

```
HOOK          → Why you're reaching out to THEM specifically
PERSONALIZATION → Something specific about their work, background, or interests
VALUE PROP    → What makes this opportunity compelling for someone like them
SOCIAL PROOF  → Brief credibility signal (traction, team, funding, mission)
CTA           → One clear, low-commitment ask
```

**Hook patterns that work:**
- Reference their specific work: "Your work on [project/contribution] caught my attention"
- Shared connection or context: "I saw your talk at [event]" / "[Mutual connection] suggested I reach out"
- Relevant trigger event: "Congrats on [achievement/milestone]"
- Industry insight: "Given your experience with [specific domain]..."

**Hook patterns that don't:**
- "I came across your profile" (generic, everyone says this)
- "I have an exciting opportunity" (self-serving, no specificity)
- "We're a fast-growing startup" (about you, not them)
- "Your skills are impressive" (empty flattery)
- "Per my last email", "reaching out", "touching base"
- "Moreover", "Furthermore", "In conclusion" (these sound like AI)

**F-Shaped Formatting:**

Structure messages like an "F" — front-load the most important content (personalization + hook) in the first 2 lines, then use shorter lines and bullet points for supporting details. Readers scan in an F-pattern — if the top-left doesn't grab them, they won't read the rest.

**Subject Line Tokens:**

Personalizing subject lines with candidate-specific tokens measurably increases open rates. Use:
- `[THEIR_NAME]` — first name only
- `[THEIR_COMPANY]` — their current employer
- `[ROLE_TITLE]` — the role you're hiring for
- `[MUTUAL_CONNECTION]` — shared contact name
- `[THEIR_CONTENT]` — reference to their post, talk, or project

## Sequence Design Principles

### Standard Sequence Structure

```
Touch 1 (Day 0)    → Primary channel, personalized first contact
Touch 2 (Day 3-5)  → Same channel, add new value or angle
Touch 3 (Day 7-10) → Switch channel, reference previous attempt
Touch 4 (Day 14)   → Breakup message, create soft urgency
```

### Timing Rules

- **Between touches:** 3-5 business days minimum
- **Best send times:** Tuesday-Thursday, 8-10am or 5-7pm candidate's timezone
- **Avoid:** Monday mornings (inbox flood), Friday afternoons, holidays, same day as company layoff announcements
- **Test weekends:** Gem data shows ~66% open rates on weekends due to less inbox competition. Schedule sends for Sunday evening / Monday 6am to land at top of inbox.
- **Best months:** January (professionals return refreshed), April (post-Q1 momentum). Q4 is weakest.
- **Seasonal:** Avoid late December, first week of January, major conference weeks

### Escalation Strategy

Each touch should add new value, not repeat:
- Touch 1: Personalized intro + value prop
- Touch 2: New angle — share a relevant insight, article, or team detail
- Touch 3: Channel switch + acknowledge persistence lightly
- Touch 4: Breakup — leave the door open, no guilt

### SOBO Strategy (Send On Behalf Of)

For leadership hires and key ICs, send the first touch from the hiring manager or team lead instead of the recruiter. Gem data (4M emails): **50%+ higher reply rates**, with some teams seeing 3-4x lifts.

**When to use SOBO:**
- Director+ and executive roles (always)
- Key IC hires (Staff+, principal, distinguished)
- Hard-to-fill roles with low response rates
- When the hiring manager has strong industry credibility

**How to implement:**
- Recruiter drafts the message, HM reviews/approves
- Send from HM's email with recruiter CC'd (or use SOBO tooling)
- HM handles the first reply; recruiter manages follow-ups
- Works for both email and LinkedIn (if HM sends the InMail)

## Personalization Hierarchy

Personalization quality directly correlates with response rate. Use the highest-available level:

| Level | Signal Type | Example | Response Lift |
|-------|------------|---------|---------------|
| **1 - Deep** | Their specific work/output | "Your Kafka consumer optimization in [repo] is elegant" | 3-5x |
| **2 - Contextual** | Career event or content | "Congrats on the Staff promo" / "Loved your post on X" | 2-3x |
| **3 - Shared** | Mutual connections/community | "[Name] suggested I reach out" / "Fellow [community] member" | 2x |
| **4 - Relevant** | Company/industry context | "Given [company]'s recent pivot to..." | 1.5x |
| **5 - Role-based** | Skills match | "Your Kubernetes + Go background is exactly what..." | 1.2x |
| **6 - None** | Template only | "We have a Senior Engineer role..." | Baseline |

For detailed research methods and signal types, see **`references/personalization-guide.md`**.

## Response Handling Framework

### Response Categories & Approach

| Response Type | Priority | Approach |
|--------------|----------|----------|
| **Interested** | Immediate | Schedule call within 48hrs, send role brief |
| **Curious but hesitant** | High | Answer questions, lower commitment ("just a chat") |
| **Not now, maybe later** | Medium | Acknowledge timing, ask permission to follow up in X months |
| **Salary question** | High | Give range if authorized, otherwise frame as "competitive for your level" |
| **Wants more info** | High | Send targeted details (not the whole JD), address their specific interest |
| **Referral offer** | Medium | Thank, ask for specific intro, offer to send role brief they can share |
| **Not interested** | Low | Thank, ask "anyone in your network?", graceful exit |
| **Objection** | Medium | Acknowledge concern, reframe with evidence |
| **Counter-offer risk** | High | Pre-seed doubt about counter-offers early in process |
| **Ghosting** | Low | One gentle follow-up, then stop |
| **Negative/rude** | None | Professional thank, immediate exit, never argue |

For complete response templates, see **`references/response-playbook.md`**.

### Key Response Principles

- **Speed matters** — respond within 24 hours, ideally same business day
- **Match their tone** — if they're casual, be casual back
- **Answer the actual question** — don't deflect to "let's hop on a call" when they asked something specific
- **Advance the conversation** — every reply should move toward a next step
- **Never be defensive** — if they object, acknowledge first, then reframe
