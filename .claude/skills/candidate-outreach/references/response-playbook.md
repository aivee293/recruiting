# Response Playbook — Templates for Every Reply Type

## General Principles

- **Reply within 24 hours** — ideally same business day. Speed signals respect.
- **Match their tone** — if they're casual, be casual back. If formal, stay formal.
- **Answer their actual question** — don't deflect to "let's hop on a call" when they asked something specific.
- **Every reply should advance** — toward a next step, even if that step is "let's revisit in 6 months."
- **Never be defensive** — if they push back, acknowledge first, then reframe.

---

## Interested / Positive Responses

### "Yes, I'm interested — tell me more"

**Priority: Immediate (within hours)**

```text
That's great to hear, [NAME]!

I'd love to set up a quick call to share more about the role and
learn about what you're looking for. Would any of these times work?

[OPTION 1]
[OPTION 2]
[OPTION 3]

Or feel free to grab a time here: [CALENDAR_LINK]

In the meantime, here's a [BRIEF_OVERVIEW / ONE-PAGER] that covers
the key details: [LINK or attached]

Looking forward to connecting.

[YOUR_NAME]
```

**Key moves:**

- Propose specific times (don't leave it open-ended)
- Share a role brief or one-pager (not the full JD yet)
- Strike while the iron is hot — don't delay

### "Interesting, but I need more info first"

**Priority: High (same day)**

```text
Absolutely — happy to share more. What specifically would be most
helpful to know? A few things candidates typically ask about:

- [TECHNICAL_SCOPE] — what the role actually involves day-to-day
- [TEAM_STRUCTURE] — who you'd work with
- [COMPENSATION_RANGE] — if helpful at this stage
- [COMPANY_CONTEXT] — where we're headed

Let me know what's top of mind and I'll send over the relevant details.

[YOUR_NAME]
```

**Key moves:**

- Don't dump everything at once — ask what they want to know
- Offering options shows transparency and respect
- If they ask about comp, see the salary question section below

## Timing / Not Ready

### "Not right now, but maybe later"

**Priority: Medium**

```text
Completely understand, [NAME] — timing is everything. I appreciate
you letting me know.

Would it be OK if I checked back in [3-6 MONTHS]? No obligation,
just want to stay on your radar in case things change.

In the meantime, feel free to reach out if you ever want to chat
about the market, comp trends, or anything else. Happy to be a
resource.

[YOUR_NAME]
```

**Key moves:**

- Ask permission to follow up (respect their boundary)
- Give a specific timeframe, not vague "sometime"
- Position yourself as a resource, not just a recruiter
- **Set a calendar reminder** to follow up when they said

### "I just started a new role"

```text
Congrats on the new role at [COMPANY], [NAME]! Hope the transition
is going well.

Totally understand the timing isn't right. Would it be OK if I
reached out in 6 months or so? These opportunities tend to come
back around.

Wishing you the best in the new position.

[YOUR_NAME]
```

## Compensation Questions

### "What's the salary / compensation?"

**Priority: High (same day)**

**If you have an approved range:**

```text
Great question. The range for this role is [RANGE_LOW]-[RANGE_HIGH]
base, plus [EQUITY/BONUS STRUCTURE]. The exact number depends on
experience and what you bring to the table.

Does that align with what you're looking for? Happy to chat through
the full comp picture on a quick call.

[YOUR_NAME]
```

**If you don't have a range yet or can't share:**

```text
Fair question — I want to be transparent. The comp is competitive
for [LEVEL] in [MARKET], benchmarked against [COMPARISON_SET —
"similar-stage startups" / "top tech companies"].

I'd rather not throw out a number without understanding your full
picture first — could we spend 15 minutes aligning on expectations?
I don't want to waste your time if we're not in the same ballpark.

[YOUR_NAME]
```

**Key principles:**

- Be as transparent as possible — candidates hate comp games
- If you have a range, share it. Withholding creates distrust.
- If you truly can't share, acknowledge it honestly and explain why
- Never lowball to "leave room for negotiation" — it backfires

### "That's below my current comp"

```text
I appreciate you sharing that, [NAME]. Compensation is important
and I don't want to waste your time.

A few things that might be relevant context:
- [EQUITY_DETAIL — if startup, equity could be significant]
- [GROWTH_DETAIL — trajectory for comp increases]
- [BENEFITS_DETAIL — any differentiators]

Would it help to chat through the full picture? If we're genuinely
too far apart, I'd rather know now — but sometimes the total package
tells a different story than base alone.

[YOUR_NAME]
```

## Objections

### "Your company is too small / too early stage"

```text
That's a fair concern, [NAME] — joining a [SIZE] company is a
real decision. A few things that might add context:

- [STABILITY_SIGNAL — runway, revenue, funding, customer base]
- [GROWTH_SIGNAL — team growth rate, recent hires from notable companies]
- [IMPACT_SIGNAL — what you'd own and influence at this stage]

The reality is [HONEST_FRAMING — "it's not for everyone, but for
the right person, the scope and impact are much larger than a big
company role"].

Worth a quick chat to see if it resonates?

[YOUR_NAME]
```

### "Your company is too big / too corporate"

```text
I get that, [NAME] — "big company" can mean different things.
A couple of things that might surprise you:

- [AUTONOMY_SIGNAL — small team within bigger org, startup-like unit]
- [CULTURE_SIGNAL — specific team culture, not corporate-wide]
- [IMPACT_SIGNAL — what this specific team ships and owns]

Happy to introduce you to [TEAM_MEMBER] who joined from
[SIMILAR_BACKGROUND — a startup, a smaller company] — they can
share their honest experience.

[YOUR_NAME]
```

### "I'm not interested in relocation / I need remote"

```text
Totally understand, [NAME]. Let me check — [FLEXIBILITY_OPTIONS]:

- [REMOTE_OPTION — "The role is actually fully remote" / "We're
  open to remote for the right person" / "We do X days in office"]
- [RELOCATION_SUPPORT — if applicable]
- [HYBRID_DETAILS — specific arrangement]

Would [SPECIFIC_ARRANGEMENT] work for you? Happy to discuss
what's flexible.

[YOUR_NAME]
```

### "I don't see how my background fits"

```text
Great question — I can see why it might not be obvious. Here's
my thinking:

Your experience with [SPECIFIC_SKILL/DOMAIN] translates directly
to [HOW_IT_MAPS]. Specifically:

- [THEIR_EXPERIENCE_1] → [HOW_IT_APPLIES]
- [THEIR_EXPERIENCE_2] → [HOW_IT_APPLIES]

The hiring manager is specifically looking for people with
[TRANSFERABLE_QUALITY] backgrounds, not just the obvious profile.

Would it help to chat with the team to see if the connection
resonates?

[YOUR_NAME]
```

## Referrals

### "I'm not interested, but I know someone who might be"

```text
I really appreciate that, [NAME] — a warm introduction would
be incredibly valuable.

Would you be comfortable:
a) Introducing us via email? (I can send a blurb you can forward)
b) Sharing their name so I can reach out and mention you suggested it?

Either way works. And thank you for thinking of someone — it
genuinely helps.

[YOUR_NAME]
```

**Follow-up: Send the forwardable blurb immediately if they choose option (a).**

### "You should talk to [Name]"

```text
Thanks for the suggestion, [NAME]! I'll reach out to [REFERRAL_NAME].

Quick question — would you be comfortable if I mentioned your name
when I contact them? A warm reference goes a long way.

Also, is there anything about [REFERRAL_NAME] I should know — what
they're great at, what motivates them, or what they might be looking for?

[YOUR_NAME]
```

## Ghosting / No Response

### After initial outreach (covered by sequence)

Follow your outreach sequence touches 2-4. Don't improvise extra follow-ups beyond what the sequence dictates.

### After they initially responded positively, then went silent

**Wait 5-7 business days, then:**

```text
Hi [NAME],

Just checking in — I know things get busy. Still interested in
connecting about the [ROLE]?

No worries either way — just let me know and I'll adjust accordingly.

[YOUR_NAME]
```

**If still no response after 7 more days:**

```text
Hi [NAME],

I'll take the silence as "not now" — totally understand. If things
change, I'm always here. Wishing you the best.

[YOUR_NAME]
```

**Key principles:**

- Maximum 2 follow-ups after ghosting (1 check-in, 1 close-out)
- Never guilt-trip or express frustration
- Leave the door open — they may come back months later

## Negative / Hostile

### Rude or angry response

```text
I apologize for the unwelcome outreach, [NAME]. I'll remove you
from any future contact.

Wishing you all the best.

[YOUR_NAME]
```

**Key principles:**

- Never argue, defend, or explain further
- One sentence apology, one sentence exit
- Remove them from ALL lists and sequences immediately
- Don't take it personally — it's rarely about you

## Counter-Offer Situations

### Candidate mentions their company might counter

**Plant doubt early (during initial conversations):**

```text
That's worth thinking about. A few things to consider:

- Counter-offers address the symptom (comp, title) but rarely
  the underlying reason you started looking
- Statistically, ~80% of people who accept counters leave within
  18 months anyway
- Your employer now knows you were looking — that can affect
  trust and future opportunities

I'm not saying don't consider it — just be honest about whether
the counter actually solves what made you open to this conversation
in the first place.
```

### Candidate received a counter-offer

```text
I appreciate you sharing that, [NAME]. Counter-offers can be
compelling — especially when your current employer values you
enough to step up.

A few questions worth sitting with:
- Does the counter address the reason you started exploring?
- How do you feel about staying now that they know you were looking?
- If you take the counter, will you be in the same spot in 12 months?

Take the time you need to make the right decision for you. I'm
here if you want to talk through it — no sales pitch, just an
honest conversation.

[YOUR_NAME]
```

**Key principles:**

- Never pressure or create urgency around counter-offers
- Help them think clearly, don't sell
- Respect their decision either way
- The relationship matters more than this one hire
