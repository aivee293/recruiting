# Universal Scorecards

Stages used for every role family: recruiter screen, behavioral, and bar raiser / culture. See `scorecard-templates.md` for the rating scale, usage steps, and the Traits Section template that must be added to every stage.

---

## 1. Recruiter Screen Scorecard (Universal)

**Format:** Phone/Video call | **Duration:** 30 min
**Interviewer:** Recruiter

### Competencies Assessed

#### 1. Role Fit & Motivation

**Definition:** Candidate's alignment with the role requirements, genuine interest in the opportunity, and clarity about what they're looking for.

| Rating | Behavioral Indicators |
|--------|----------------------|
| 5 -- Strong Yes | Clear articulation of why this role and company. Career goals directly align. Demonstrates deep knowledge of the company/product. Can explain what specifically drew them to this opportunity. |
| 4 -- Yes | Genuine and specific interest in the role. Career trajectory aligns well. Has researched the company and can reference specifics. Motivation is clearly tied to the opportunity, not just a job search. |
| 3 -- Mixed | Genuine interest with reasonable alignment. Career trajectory makes sense for this role. Has done some research on the company. Motivation seems authentic. |
| 2 -- No | Vague or generic interest. Can't articulate why this company vs competitors. Career goals are unclear or misaligned. Applying broadly without strong specific interest. |
| 1 -- Strong No | No knowledge of or interest in the company. Clearly looking for something different. Motivation is purely transactional with no role alignment. |

**Questions:**

1. What drew you to this opportunity, and what are you looking for in your next role? → Probe: What specifically about [company/team/product] interests you?
2. Walk me through your current situation — what's prompting you to explore? → Probe: What would make you say no to an opportunity?

**Evidence to capture:** Specific reasons cited for interest, career goals mentioned, deal-breakers stated, questions they ask about the role.

#### 2. Must-Have Qualification Check

**Definition:** Binary verification that the candidate meets the non-negotiable requirements for the role (experience, skills, certifications, location, etc.).

| Rating | Behavioral Indicators |
|--------|----------------------|
| 5 -- Strong Yes | Exceeds all must-haves. Experience directly maps to role requirements. No qualification gaps. |
| 4 -- Yes | Meets all must-haves solidly. Experience is relevant and covers each requirement. No concerns about qualification fit. |
| 3 -- Mixed | Meets all must-haves. May have minor gaps that are easily addressed. Experience is relevant if not identical. |
| 2 -- No | Missing one must-have that could potentially be overcome. Borderline on key qualifications. Requires hiring manager judgment. |
| 1 -- Strong No | Missing multiple must-haves. Experience doesn't map to role requirements. Disqualifying gap identified. |

**Questions:**

1. [Role-specific must-have question] — Looking for: [specific criteria]
2. [Role-specific must-have question] — Looking for: [specific criteria]
3. [Location/availability/work authorization as applicable]

**Evidence to capture:** Pass/fail on each must-have, specific experience cited, any gaps identified.

#### 3. Communication & Presence

**Definition:** How clearly and concisely the candidate communicates, their energy level, and professional presence in a conversational setting.

| Rating | Behavioral Indicators |
|--------|----------------------|
| 5 -- Strong Yes | Exceptionally clear and concise. Structures thoughts well. Engages naturally. Asks insightful questions. Adapts communication style. |
| 4 -- Yes | Communicates clearly and directly. Answers are well-organized. Professional and engaged. Asks thoughtful questions about the role. |
| 3 -- Mixed | Communicates clearly. Answers questions directly. Professional demeanor. Reasonable questions about the role. |
| 2 -- No | Rambling or unfocused answers. Difficulty getting to the point. Low energy or engagement. Doesn't ask questions. |
| 1 -- Strong No | Cannot communicate basic ideas clearly. Unprofessional behavior. Disengaged. Concerning red flags (badmouthing employers, dishonesty). |

**Questions:** Evaluated throughout the conversation — no specific questions needed.

**Evidence to capture:** Communication quality, notable quotes, energy/enthusiasm level, questions they asked.

#### 4. Compensation & Logistics Alignment

**Definition:** Alignment between the candidate's expectations and the role's parameters — compensation, start date, location, work model.

| Rating | Behavioral Indicators |
|--------|----------------------|
| 5 -- Strong Yes | Expectations fully within range. Available on timeline. Location and work model fully aligned. No competing offers creating urgency pressure. |
| 4 -- Yes | Expectations within range. Timeline and logistics work with minor coordination. No significant barriers to alignment. |
| 3 -- Mixed | Expectations within range or slightly flexible. Minor logistics to work through. Manageable timeline. |
| 2 -- No | Expectations at or above top of range. Significant logistics concerns. Tight timeline due to competing offers. Relocation hesitancy. |
| 1 -- Strong No | Expectations far above range with no flexibility. Unavailable on needed timeline. Hard location/work model incompatibility. |

**Questions:**

1. What are your compensation expectations for this type of role? → Probe: Is that flexible depending on the total package?
2. What does your timeline look like? Are you exploring other opportunities? → Probe: Any deadlines we should be aware of?

**Evidence to capture:** Stated comp expectations, other processes/offers, availability, location constraints, flexibility signals.

### Stage Recommendation

- [ ] 5 -- Strong Yes: Advance immediately and prioritize scheduling
- [ ] 4 -- Yes: Advance to next stage
- [ ] 3 -- Mixed: Flag areas of uncertainty, may still advance
- [ ] 2 -- No: Do not advance (document specific gaps)
- [ ] 1 -- Strong No: Do not advance

## 4. Behavioral Interview Scorecard (Universal)

**Format:** Video / in-person | **Duration:** 45-60 min
**Interviewer:** Hiring Manager or Senior Team Member

### Competencies Assessed

#### 1. Collaboration & Influence

**Definition:** Ability to work effectively across teams, resolve conflict, and influence outcomes without relying on authority.

| Rating | Behavioral Indicators |
|--------|----------------------|
| 5 -- Strong Yes | Provides specific examples of cross-functional collaboration with concrete outcomes. Navigates conflict constructively — addresses issues directly while maintaining relationships. Influences through data, empathy, and shared goals. Shows pattern of building trust across teams. |
| 4 -- Yes | Gives clear examples of collaboration with measurable outcomes. Handles conflict directly and professionally. Can influence peers through reasoning and relationship-building. Works effectively across team boundaries. |
| 3 -- Mixed | Gives reasonable examples of collaboration. Handles conflict adequately. Can influence others when needed. Works well in team settings. |
| 2 -- No | Examples are vague or limited to within their own team. Avoids conflict or handles it poorly. Relies on authority or escalation rather than influence. Limited cross-functional experience. |
| 1 -- Strong No | Cannot provide collaboration examples. Blames others in conflict situations. Described by behavior as difficult to work with. No ability to influence without authority. |

**Questions:**

1. Tell me about a time you had to work with a team or person who had fundamentally different priorities. → Probe: How did you find common ground?
2. Describe a conflict on a project. What was your role in resolving it? → Probe: What would you do differently?

**Evidence to capture:** Specific examples cited, how they describe other people, resolution approaches used, outcomes achieved.

#### 2. Ownership & Accountability

**Definition:** Taking responsibility for outcomes, going beyond defined scope when needed, and following through on commitments.

| Rating | Behavioral Indicators |
|--------|----------------------|
| 5 -- Strong Yes | Takes ownership proactively — doesn't wait to be assigned. Sees problems and fixes them even outside their scope. Follows through on commitments even when difficult. Takes responsibility for failures. Shows initiative repeatedly (pattern, not one-off). |
| 4 -- Yes | Consistently owns their work end-to-end. Steps beyond defined scope when the situation calls for it. Reliable on commitments. Takes responsibility for outcomes, including setbacks. Shows initiative as a regular behavior. |
| 3 -- Mixed | Reliable on commitments. Willing to stretch beyond defined role when asked. Takes responsibility for their work. Reasonable follow-through. |
| 2 -- No | Sticks rigidly to defined scope. Inconsistent follow-through. Deflects responsibility subtly ("the team decided" when it was their call). Needs to be reminded about commitments. |
| 1 -- Strong No | Blames others for failures. Drops commitments without communication. Refuses work outside narrow scope. No examples of initiative. |

**Questions:**

1. Tell me about a time you took ownership of something that wasn't technically your responsibility. → Probe: What would have happened if you hadn't stepped in?
2. Describe a commitment you made that turned out much harder than expected. → Probe: At what point did you realize, and what did you do?

**Evidence to capture:** Scope of ownership shown, how they describe their role vs the team's, follow-through examples, accountability language.

#### 3. Growth Mindset & Self-Awareness

**Definition:** Ability to learn from mistakes, receive feedback constructively, and accurately assess their own strengths and weaknesses.

| Rating | Behavioral Indicators |
|--------|----------------------|
| 5 -- Strong Yes | Provides genuine examples of failure and growth. Describes feedback that changed their behavior. Shows accurate self-assessment (strengths AND weaknesses). Demonstrates curiosity and continuous learning. Comfortable with "I don't know." |
| 4 -- Yes | Shares specific examples of learning from mistakes. Receives and acts on feedback. Self-assessment is realistic and balanced. Shows a pattern of skill development and adaptation. |
| 3 -- Mixed | Can describe lessons learned from experience. Accepts feedback. Reasonable self-awareness. Shows willingness to grow. |
| 2 -- No | Failures are always external. Feedback examples are superficial. Self-assessment is inaccurate (all strengths or performative weaknesses). Limited evidence of growth or adaptation. |
| 1 -- Strong No | No acknowledgment of failures or weaknesses. Defensive about feedback. Rigid thinking. Blames circumstances for every setback. |

**Questions:**

1. Tell me about a time you were wrong about something important. What did you learn? → Probe: How did it change your approach?
2. What feedback have you received that was hardest to hear? → Probe: What did you do with it?

**Evidence to capture:** Quality of self-reflection, specificity of lessons learned, how they talk about failure, growth trajectory.

#### 4. Adaptability

**Definition:** How effectively the candidate handles change, ambiguity, and competing priorities.

| Rating | Behavioral Indicators |
|--------|----------------------|
| 5 -- Strong Yes | Thrives in ambiguous situations — creates structure where there is none. Pivots quickly when priorities shift. Learns new domains rapidly. Maintains effectiveness under pressure. Turns ambiguity into opportunity. |
| 4 -- Yes | Handles ambiguity well and can create a path forward. Adjusts priorities effectively when circumstances change. Learns new areas at a good pace. Stays productive under pressure. |
| 3 -- Mixed | Handles change and ambiguity adequately. Adjusts priorities when asked. Learns new things at a reasonable pace. Functions under moderate pressure. |
| 2 -- No | Struggles with ambiguity — needs clear direction. Slow to adapt to changing priorities. Gets flustered when plans change. Limited examples of working outside comfort zone. |
| 1 -- Strong No | Paralyzed by ambiguity. Resists change actively. Cannot function without detailed instructions. Falls apart under pressure. |

**Questions:**

1. Tell me about a time your priorities shifted significantly mid-project. → Probe: How quickly did you adjust? What did you deprioritize?
2. Describe a situation where you had to work with significant ambiguity. → Probe: How did you decide what to focus on?

**Evidence to capture:** How they describe change (positive/negative framing), specific adaptations made, pace of adjustment, comfort level with ambiguity.

### Stage Recommendation

- [ ] 5 -- Strong Yes: Advance immediately and prioritize scheduling
- [ ] 4 -- Yes: Advance to next stage
- [ ] 3 -- Mixed: Flag areas of uncertainty, may still advance
- [ ] 2 -- No: Do not advance (document specific gaps)
- [ ] 1 -- Strong No: Do not advance

## 10. Bar Raiser / Culture Scorecard (Universal)

**Format:** Video / in-person behavioral interview | **Duration:** 45-60 min
**Interviewer:** Senior leader outside the hiring team (independent evaluator)

> **Note on traits:** This template assesses culture through generic skill-like competencies (Values Alignment, Raising the Bar, etc.). When using Teamtailor, replace the "Values Alignment" competency with individual company traits from the culture/values deck referenced by `culture-value-deck` in `.claude/talent-sourcing.local.md`, each rated separately. The other competencies (Raising the Bar, Long-Term Potential, Judgment & Integrity) remain as skills (blue). This gives you per-trait Job Match Score data instead of a single "values alignment" rating.

### Competencies Assessed

#### 1. Values Alignment

**Definition:** Alignment with the company's core values — demonstrated through past behavior, not stated beliefs.

| Rating | Behavioral Indicators |
|--------|----------------------|
| 5 -- Strong Yes | Provides specific behavioral examples that demonstrate company values in action. Values show up as a pattern across multiple examples (not a one-off). Can describe situations where values were in tension and how they navigated the trade-off. Values are genuine (consistent across examples), not performed for the interview. |
| 4 -- Yes | Gives behavioral examples that align with company values. Values appear consistent across different stories. Shows genuine alignment rather than rehearsed answers. Can discuss values trade-offs with real examples. |
| 3 -- Mixed | Shows alignment with key values through examples. Behavior generally consistent with values. Reasonable fit. |
| 2 -- No | Values alignment is surface-level. Examples don't demonstrate actual behavior change. Some misalignment with key values. Answers feel rehearsed rather than authentic. |
| 1 -- Strong No | Clear values misalignment. Behavior patterns contradict core values. Described actions that would be problematic in the company's culture. |

**Questions:**

1. What does [company value] mean to you? Give me an example from your work. → Probe: What did it cost you to live that value? Was there a time it was hard?
2. Tell me about a time your personal values conflicted with what your company or team wanted. → Probe: How did you resolve it? What did you learn?

**Evidence to capture:** Specific behavioral examples per value, consistency across stories, authenticity signals, areas of alignment and misalignment.

#### 2. Raising the Bar

**Definition:** Will this candidate raise the average on the team? Would you want to work with or for this person?

| Rating | Behavioral Indicators |
|--------|----------------------|
| 5 -- Strong Yes | Demonstrates excellence in their domain that would elevate the team. Brings skills, perspectives, or approaches the team currently lacks. Would make other team members better through collaboration. Sets a high standard for their own work. Evidence of raising the bar in previous roles. |
| 4 -- Yes | Strong in their domain and would contribute meaningfully to the team. Brings relevant skills and perspectives. Collaborates in ways that improve group output. Holds themselves to a high standard. |
| 3 -- Mixed | Solid contributor who would meet the team's current bar. Brings relevant experience. Would integrate well. Competent in their domain. |
| 2 -- No | Would meet the current bar but not raise it. No differentiated skills or perspectives. Adequate but not additive. Team wouldn't be noticeably better or worse. |
| 1 -- Strong No | Would lower the team's bar. Skill level below current team average. Would not make others better. Standards are lower than the team's current expectations. |

**Questions:**

1. What would your colleagues say you bring to a team that nobody else does? → Probe: Give me a specific example where that unique contribution mattered.
2. How do you make the people around you better at their jobs? → Probe: Can you give me a concrete example?

**Evidence to capture:** Unique contributions described, evidence of elevating others, skill level relative to team, differentiated perspectives or approaches.

#### 3. Long-Term Potential

**Definition:** Trajectory and growth potential — not just where they are today, but where they're headed. Can this person grow with the company?

| Rating | Behavioral Indicators |
|--------|----------------------|
| 5 -- Strong Yes | Clear growth trajectory visible in career history. Increasing scope, complexity, and impact over time. Demonstrates learning agility — picks up new domains and skills quickly. Ambitious but self-aware about development areas. Has a thoughtful perspective on where they want to grow. |
| 4 -- Yes | Positive career trajectory with increasing responsibility. Shows learning agility and adaptability. Career goals align with what the company offers. Self-aware about strengths and development areas. |
| 3 -- Mixed | Reasonable career trajectory. Some evidence of growth. Has career goals that align with what the company can offer. Willing to develop. |
| 2 -- No | Flat trajectory. Limited growth evidence. Career goals unclear or misaligned with company opportunities. May plateau quickly. |
| 1 -- Strong No | Declining trajectory. No evidence of growth or learning. Career goals fundamentally misaligned. Likely to leave quickly or stagnate. |

**Questions:**

1. Looking at your career arc, what's the thread that connects your moves? Where are you heading? → Probe: What do you need to develop to get there?
2. Tell me about the steepest learning curve you've been on. How did you ramp up? → Probe: What's the most recent thing you've learned that changed how you work?

**Evidence to capture:** Career trajectory observations, growth evidence, learning agility signals, alignment between candidate's goals and company opportunities.

#### 4. Judgment & Integrity

**Definition:** Quality of decision-making in gray areas — ethical reasoning, intellectual honesty, and trustworthiness.

| Rating | Behavioral Indicators |
|--------|----------------------|
| 5 -- Strong Yes | Demonstrates sound judgment in ambiguous ethical situations. Takes the harder right path over the easier wrong one. Intellectually honest — acknowledges mistakes, uncertainty, and trade-offs. Trustworthy — consistent behavior across examples. Considers impact on others in their decisions. |
| 4 -- Yes | Shows sound judgment in difficult situations. Honest about mistakes and limitations. Makes ethical choices even when inconvenient. Consistent and trustworthy based on examples. Considers broader impact in decision-making. |
| 3 -- Mixed | Reasonable judgment. Honest about strengths and weaknesses. Considers ethical dimensions of decisions. Trustworthy based on examples shared. |
| 2 -- No | Judgment is inconsistent or questionable in some areas. Some intellectual dishonesty (exaggerating, minimizing mistakes). Ethical reasoning is underdeveloped. |
| 1 -- Strong No | Concerning judgment calls described. Dishonesty detected (inconsistencies, embellishments that don't hold up). Ethical blind spots. Would not trust this person in a gray area. |

**Questions:**

1. Tell me about a time you faced an ethical dilemma at work. What did you do? → Probe: What was the cost of doing the right thing?
2. When have you disagreed with your boss or company on something important? How did you handle it? → Probe: What would you have done if they didn't listen?

**Evidence to capture:** Ethical reasoning quality, consistency across examples, intellectual honesty signals, trustworthiness assessment.

### Stage Recommendation

- [ ] 2 -- No: Do not advance (document specific gaps)
- [ ] 1 -- Strong No: Do not advance
