## 8. Portfolio Review Scorecard (Design)

**Format:** Candidate-led presentation + discussion | **Duration:** 60 min
**Interviewer:** Design Lead + 1 team member

### Competencies Assessed

#### 1. Design Process & Thinking

**Definition:** How the designer approaches problems — from understanding the problem through to delivering solutions. Process rigor, not just output quality.

| Rating | Behavioral Indicators |
|--------|----------------------|
| 5 -- Strong Yes | Clear, repeatable process from research through delivery. Adapts process to problem scope (doesn't over-process simple problems). Shows how research informed decisions. Iterates based on feedback and data. Can explain WHY they made specific design choices, not just WHAT they made. |
| 4 -- Yes | Defined process that includes research, ideation, and iteration. Adapts approach to the problem. Research clearly informs key decisions. Explains design rationale with specifics. |
| 3 -- Mixed | Reasonable design process. Shows research and iteration. Can explain major design decisions. Process is appropriate for the problem scope. |
| 2 -- No | Process is unclear or inconsistent. Jumps from problem to solution without visible research. Can't explain why specific design decisions were made. Portfolio shows outputs, not thinking. |
| 1 -- Strong No | No evidence of design process. Cannot articulate how they approach problems. Designs based on aesthetics alone without user or business rationale. |

**Questions:**

1. Walk me through this project from the beginning — how did you understand the problem? → Probe: What did you learn from research that surprised you or changed your direction?
2. Show me a design decision you debated. What were the options, and why did you choose this one? → Probe: How did you validate it?

**Evidence to capture:** Process steps described, research methods used, how decisions were justified, iteration evidence.

#### 2. Visual & Interaction Quality

**Definition:** Craft quality of the design work — visual design, interaction design, attention to detail, and consistency.

| Rating | Behavioral Indicators |
|--------|----------------------|
| 5 -- Strong Yes | Polished, professional visual design. Thoughtful interaction patterns that feel intuitive. Consistent design language. Attention to detail (spacing, typography, color, motion). Designs feel coherent and intentional. Accessibility considerations visible. |
| 4 -- Yes | Strong visual design with clear intention. Interaction patterns work well and feel considered. Consistent design language across the project. Good attention to detail. Accessibility is addressed. |
| 3 -- Mixed | Good visual design. Interactions work well. Reasonable consistency. Competent craft. |
| 2 -- No | Visual design is inconsistent or unrefined. Interactions are confusing or standard without thought. Lack of attention to detail. Inconsistent design language. |
| 1 -- Strong No | Poor visual design fundamentals. Interactions don't make sense. No consistency. No evidence of craft development. |

**Questions:**

1. Walk me through the visual design decisions on this project — type, color, layout. → Probe: How did you ensure consistency across the experience?
2. Tell me about the interaction design — how does the user flow through this? → Probe: How do you handle edge cases (errors, empty states, loading)?

**Evidence to capture:** Visual quality assessment, interaction design quality, attention to detail, consistency, accessibility.

#### 3. User Empathy & Research Translation

**Definition:** Understanding of users — how research and user insight translates into design decisions.

| Rating | Behavioral Indicators |
|--------|----------------------|
| 5 -- Strong Yes | Deep user empathy grounded in research (not assumption). Can trace specific design decisions back to user insights. Designs for diverse user needs and contexts. Tests designs with users and iterates. Advocates for the user while balancing business needs. |
| 4 -- Yes | User empathy is informed by research. Key design decisions link to user insights. Considers different user contexts and needs. Has tested designs with users and adjusted based on findings. |
| 3 -- Mixed | Shows user awareness. Some research informs designs. Can explain who the user is and what they need. Some evidence of user testing. |
| 2 -- No | Designs based on assumptions about users. Limited or no research. Can't describe the target user clearly. No evidence of user testing or iteration. |
| 1 -- Strong No | No user awareness. Designs for themselves. Cannot describe who uses the product or why. No evidence of empathy in design decisions. |

**Questions:**

1. Who was this designed for? How do you know what they need? → Probe: What user insight most influenced this design?
2. How did you test this with users? What did you learn and change? → Probe: What surprised you?

**Evidence to capture:** User research methods, insights cited, design changes based on research, user advocacy examples.

#### 4. Business Impact & Stakeholder Communication

**Definition:** Ability to connect design work to business outcomes and communicate effectively with non-design stakeholders.

| Rating | Behavioral Indicators |
|--------|----------------------|
| 5 -- Strong Yes | Can articulate business impact of their design work (metrics, outcomes). Presents design rationale in business terms. Effectively manages stakeholder feedback without compromising design quality. Negotiates scope and trade-offs. Measured results. |
| 4 -- Yes | Connects design work to business metrics. Communicates design rationale in terms stakeholders understand. Manages stakeholder feedback effectively, knowing when to push back and when to adapt. Tracks outcomes. |
| 3 -- Mixed | Some awareness of business impact. Communicates design decisions clearly. Manages stakeholder feedback reasonably. |
| 2 -- No | Cannot connect design to business outcomes. Presents design in design jargon only. Rolls over on stakeholder feedback or dismisses it. |
| 1 -- Strong No | No awareness of business impact. Cannot present to non-designers. Adversarial relationship with stakeholders. Design exists in a vacuum. |

**Questions:**

1. What was the business impact of this project? How do you know? → Probe: What metrics did you track?
2. Tell me about a time a stakeholder pushed back on your design. How did you handle it? → Probe: What did you change vs push back on?

**Evidence to capture:** Metrics or outcomes cited, stakeholder management approach, how they balance design quality with business needs.

- [ ] 4 -- Yes: Advance to next stage
- [ ] 3 -- Mixed: Flag areas of uncertainty, may still advance
- [ ] 2 -- No: Do not advance (document specific gaps)
- [ ] 1 -- Strong No: Do not advance
