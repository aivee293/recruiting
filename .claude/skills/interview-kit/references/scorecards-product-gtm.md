## 6. Case Study Scorecard (Product / GTM)

**Format:** Presentation + discussion | **Duration:** 60 min
**Interviewer:** Hiring Manager + 1-2 team members

### Competencies Assessed

#### 1. Analytical Rigor

**Definition:** Quality of analysis — structuring the problem, using data appropriately, and drawing defensible conclusions.

| Rating | Behavioral Indicators |
|--------|----------------------|
| 5 -- Strong Yes | Structures the problem clearly before diving into analysis. Uses data to support every conclusion. Identifies what data is missing and how they'd get it. Distinguishes correlation from causation. Quantifies impact where possible. |
| 4 -- Yes | Structures the problem logically. Uses data to support key conclusions. Acknowledges data gaps. Conclusions are sound and well-supported. Quantifies impact for the main recommendation. |
| 3 -- Mixed | Reasonable problem structure. Uses available data. Conclusions follow from evidence. Some quantification of impact. |
| 2 -- No | Jumps to conclusions without supporting data. Problem structure is unclear. Misinterprets data. Conclusions don't follow from analysis. |
| 1 -- Strong No | No analytical framework. Ignores available data. Conclusions are pure opinion. Cannot structure a problem. |

**Questions:**

1. Walk me through how you approached this case. What did you consider? → Probe: What data would you want that you didn't have?
2. How confident are you in your recommendation? What would change your mind? → Probe: What's the biggest risk in your approach?

**Evidence to capture:** Problem structure, data usage, quality of conclusions, risk awareness, intellectual honesty about gaps.

#### 2. Strategic Thinking

**Definition:** Ability to see the big picture, connect the case to business strategy, and think about long-term implications.

| Rating | Behavioral Indicators |
|--------|----------------------|
| 5 -- Strong Yes | Connects the case to broader business strategy. Considers competitive dynamics. Thinks about second-order effects. Balances short-term wins with long-term positioning. Prioritizes based on strategic impact, not just tactical ease. |
| 4 -- Yes | Shows clear strategic awareness. Connects the recommendation to business context. Considers competitive positioning. Prioritizes based on impact. Thinks beyond the immediate case. |
| 3 -- Mixed | Shows awareness of broader context. Makes reasonable strategic connections. Considers competitive landscape. Prioritization is sensible. |
| 2 -- No | Focuses only on the immediate problem. No connection to strategy. Ignores competitive context. Prioritization is tactical only. |
| 1 -- Strong No | Cannot think beyond the immediate case. No strategic awareness. Recommendations are disconnected from business reality. |

**Questions:**

1. How does your recommendation fit into the company's broader strategy? → Probe: What would a competitor do in response?
2. If you could only do one thing from your recommendation, what would it be and why? → Probe: How would you sequence the rest?

**Evidence to capture:** Strategic connections made, competitive awareness, prioritization reasoning, long-term thinking.

#### 3. Communication & Persuasion

**Definition:** Clarity and persuasiveness of the presentation — can they tell a compelling story with their analysis?

| Rating | Behavioral Indicators |
|--------|----------------------|
| 5 -- Strong Yes | Clear narrative arc. Leads with the recommendation. Supporting evidence is well-organized. Handles questions with poise. Adjusts depth based on audience. Visuals enhance rather than distract. |
| 4 -- Yes | Clear and logical presentation. Recommendation is front and center. Evidence is organized and easy to follow. Handles questions confidently. Appropriate level of detail for the audience. |
| 3 -- Mixed | Clear presentation. Recommendation is understandable. Handles most questions. Reasonable visual aids. |
| 2 -- No | Presentation is disorganized. Recommendation is buried. Flustered by tough questions. Too much or too little detail. |
| 1 -- Strong No | Cannot present coherently. No clear recommendation. Falls apart under questioning. Reads from notes/slides without adding insight. |

**Questions:** Evaluated throughout the presentation and Q&A.

**Evidence to capture:** Presentation structure, clarity of recommendation, response to challenges, audience awareness.

#### 4. Customer & Market Awareness

**Definition:** Understanding of customer needs, market dynamics, and user behavior relevant to the case.

| Rating | Behavioral Indicators |
|--------|----------------------|
| 5 -- Strong Yes | Centers the analysis on customer/user needs. References market data or trends. Segments users and considers different needs. Balances customer value with business value. Shows genuine customer empathy. |
| 4 -- Yes | Customer perspective is a clear input to the analysis. Aware of relevant market dynamics. Considers different user segments. Balances user needs with business goals. |
| 3 -- Mixed | Considers the customer perspective. Some market awareness. Reasonable understanding of user needs. |
| 2 -- No | Analysis is internally focused (org, technology, process). Customer is an afterthought. No market context. One-size-fits-all approach to users. |
| 1 -- Strong No | Ignores customer needs entirely. No awareness of market context. Recommendations would harm the user experience. |

**Questions:**

1. How did you think about the end user in your analysis? → Probe: Which user segment benefits most from your recommendation?
2. What market trends or competitive dynamics did you factor in? → Probe: How would this look different in 2 years?

**Evidence to capture:** Customer/user references, market knowledge demonstrated, segmentation thinking, empathy signals.

### Stage Recommendation

- [ ] 5 -- Strong Yes: Advance immediately and prioritize scheduling
- [ ] 4 -- Yes: Advance to next stage
- [ ] 3 -- Mixed: Flag areas of uncertainty, may still advance
- [ ] 2 -- No: Do not advance (document specific gaps)
- [ ] 1 -- Strong No: Do not advance

## 9. Sales Role Play Scorecard (GTM)

**Format:** Simulated customer call or demo | **Duration:** 45-60 min
**Interviewer:** Sales Manager + 1 team member (playing customer)

### Competencies Assessed

#### 1. Discovery & Qualification

**Definition:** Ability to uncover customer pain, qualify the opportunity, and understand the buying process through effective questioning.

| Rating | Behavioral Indicators |
|--------|----------------------|
| 5 -- Strong Yes | Asks layered questions that uncover root pain (not just surface symptoms). Qualifies budget, authority, need, and timeline naturally — doesn't sound like a checklist. Listens actively — builds on what the customer says. Identifies the compelling event driving urgency. Maps the buying committee. |
| 4 -- Yes | Asks probing questions that go beyond surface level. Qualifies the opportunity on key dimensions. Listens well and adapts questions based on answers. Identifies the main pain points and urgency drivers. Understands the decision-making process. |
| 3 -- Mixed | Asks good qualifying questions. Uncovers the main pain points. Listens and adapts. Gets basic qualification information. |
| 2 -- No | Questions are superficial or scripted. Doesn't dig below the surface. Misses qualifying signals. Moves to pitching too quickly. |
| 1 -- Strong No | No discovery — goes straight to pitch. Cannot ask open-ended questions. Doesn't listen to answers. Talks more than the customer. |

**Questions:** Evaluated during the role play. Brief: "The customer is [persona]. They reached out because [trigger]. Discover their needs and determine if this is a qualified opportunity."

**Evidence to capture:** Question quality, listening signals, qualification information gathered, how they built rapport.

#### 2. Solution Positioning & Storytelling

**Definition:** Connecting the solution to the customer's specific pain — telling a compelling story, not reciting features.

| Rating | Behavioral Indicators |
|--------|----------------------|
| 5 -- Strong Yes | Tailors the pitch to discovered pain (not a canned demo). Uses customer's language and priorities. Tells relevant customer stories. Differentiates from alternatives naturally. Quantifies value in the customer's terms. Makes the customer see themselves using the solution. |
| 4 -- Yes | Adapts the pitch based on what they learned in discovery. Uses the customer's language. Tells a clear value story. Differentiates from alternatives. Connects features to the customer's specific situation. |
| 3 -- Mixed | Connects features to needs. Uses some customer language. Reasonable differentiation. Tells the product story clearly. |
| 2 -- No | Generic pitch regardless of discovery. Feature-dumping. Doesn't connect to customer pain. Weak differentiation. |
| 1 -- Strong No | Recites features. Ignores what the customer said they need. Cannot differentiate from competitors. No storytelling ability. |

**Questions:** Evaluated during the role play. Look for: Did they tailor the pitch based on what they learned in discovery?

**Evidence to capture:** How they connected solution to pain, customer language used, stories told, differentiation approach.

#### 3. Objection Handling

**Definition:** Responding to customer concerns, objections, and pushback — turning resistance into progression.

| Rating | Behavioral Indicators |
|--------|----------------------|
| 5 -- Strong Yes | Acknowledges objections without being defensive. Asks clarifying questions to understand the real concern. Reframes objections as opportunities. Uses proof points (case studies, data) to address concerns. Maintains confidence without being dismissive. Handles pricing objections with value framing. |
| 4 -- Yes | Acknowledges objections and addresses them directly. Asks follow-up questions to understand the concern. Uses relevant proof points. Maintains composure and confidence. Advances the conversation after handling each objection. |
| 3 -- Mixed | Handles common objections adequately. Doesn't get flustered. Provides reasonable responses. Maintains composure. |
| 2 -- No | Defensive when challenged. Steamrolls objections. One-size-fits-all responses. Gets flustered by unexpected pushback. |
| 1 -- Strong No | Cannot handle objections. Becomes argumentative. Gives up at first pushback. Makes promises they can't keep. Panic-discounts. |

**Questions:** The role-play "customer" should raise 2-3 objections: price, timing, competitive alternative, internal resistance. Observe how the candidate handles each.

**Evidence to capture:** Objection handling approach, composure, proof points used, whether they advanced the conversation after each objection.

#### 4. Next Steps & Deal Progression

**Definition:** Moving the deal forward — creating urgency, securing commitment, and establishing a clear path to close.

| Rating | Behavioral Indicators |
|--------|----------------------|
| 5 -- Strong Yes | Proposes a specific next step (not "I'll follow up"). Gets commitment to a date and action. Creates urgency tied to the customer's compelling event. Identifies and plans for potential blockers. Maps what's needed to get to a decision. Multi-threads (plans to engage other stakeholders). |
| 4 -- Yes | Proposes a concrete next step with a timeline. Gets commitment from the customer. Ties urgency to the customer's needs. Plans for the next interaction with clear objectives. Considers who else needs to be involved. |
| 3 -- Mixed | Asks for next steps. Gets some commitment. Reasonable sense of urgency. Plans for the next meeting. |
| 2 -- No | Vague next steps ("let's stay in touch"). No urgency creation. Doesn't ask for commitment. No plan to engage other stakeholders. |
| 1 -- Strong No | No attempt to advance the deal. Leaves the ball in the customer's court. No urgency. No follow-up plan. |

**Questions:** Observe how they close the role play. Did they propose a specific next step? Did they get commitment?

**Evidence to capture:** Next step proposed, commitment obtained, urgency tactics, deal progression plan.

#### 5. Professionalism & Rapport

**Definition:** Building trust and relationship — the intangibles that make a customer want to work with this person.

| Rating | Behavioral Indicators |
|--------|----------------------|
| 5 -- Strong Yes | Builds rapport quickly and authentically. Professional but not stiff. Confident without being arrogant. Adapts style to the customer's personality. Makes the customer feel heard and valued. Would represent the company well. |
| 4 -- Yes | Professional and personable. Builds rapport naturally. Confident and composed. Adapts to the customer's communication style. Would represent the company well in customer-facing situations. |
| 3 -- Mixed | Professional and personable. Reasonable rapport. Represents the company adequately. Customer would take a second meeting. |
| 2 -- No | Awkward or overly scripted. Poor rapport. Too casual or too formal for the context. Customer wouldn't feel confident in this person. |
| 1 -- Strong No | Unprofessional behavior. Arrogant or dismissive. Poor listening. Would damage the company's reputation. |

**Questions:** Evaluated throughout the role play. Pay attention to the "feel" of the interaction — would you buy from this person?

**Evidence to capture:** Rapport quality, adaptability to customer style, professionalism observations, trust signals.
