## 7. Leadership Assessment Scorecard (Manager+)

**Format:** Video / in-person behavioral interview | **Duration:** 60 min
**Interviewer:** Skip-level leader or VP

### Competencies Assessed

#### 1. People Development

**Definition:** Track record of hiring, developing, and growing talent — building teams that are stronger over time.

| Rating | Behavioral Indicators |
|--------|----------------------|
| 5 -- Strong Yes | Specific examples of people they've developed with measurable outcomes (promotions, expanded scope, skill growth). Deliberate approach to talent development — not just "I hire great people." Invests in underperformers with clear expectations and timelines. Has made tough people decisions when needed. Team members would want to work for them again. |
| 4 -- Yes | Clear examples of developing team members with tangible results. Has a structured approach to talent development. Has addressed underperformance directly with defined outcomes. Hires thoughtfully with a repeatable process. |
| 3 -- Mixed | Has developed team members with some evidence. Approach to development is reasonable. Has handled underperformance. Hires thoughtfully. |
| 2 -- No | Development examples are vague or limited. Avoids tough people conversations. Hiring approach is unstructured. "I get out of their way" without actual development. |
| 1 -- Strong No | No evidence of developing anyone. Has never managed underperformance. Hiring is based on gut feel. Takes credit for team's innate talent. |

**Questions:**

1. Tell me about someone you developed significantly. What did you do? → Probe: Where are they now? What would they say you did for them?
2. Walk me through how you've handled an underperformer. → Probe: How early did you address it? What was the outcome?
3. What's your approach to hiring? Tell me about a great hire and one that didn't work out. → Probe: What did you learn from the miss?

**Evidence to capture:** Specific development examples with outcomes, approach to underperformance, hiring philosophy, tough decisions made.

#### 2. Strategic Leadership

**Definition:** Setting direction, making tough resource allocation decisions, and connecting team work to business outcomes.

| Rating | Behavioral Indicators |
|--------|----------------------|
| 5 -- Strong Yes | Has set strategy that drove measurable business outcomes. Makes resource allocation trade-offs explicitly. Connects team's work to company strategy. Thinks in systems — understands second-order effects. Balances short-term delivery with long-term capability building. |
| 4 -- Yes | Sets clear direction for the team tied to business goals. Makes resource trade-off decisions with reasoning. Connects team work to company objectives. Balances delivery with capability building. |
| 3 -- Mixed | Sets reasonable direction for team. Makes trade-off decisions. Understands how team's work connects to business. Some evidence of strategic thinking. |
| 2 -- No | Direction is reactive (responding to stakeholders, not setting agenda). Avoids resource trade-off decisions. Weak connection between team work and business outcomes. Tactical manager, not strategic leader. |
| 1 -- Strong No | No evidence of setting direction. Cannot explain how their team's work connects to business outcomes. Purely execution-focused with no strategic perspective. |

**Questions:**

1. What's the most impactful strategic decision you've made for your team or organization? → Probe: What did you say no to in order to say yes to that?
2. How do you balance short-term delivery pressure with long-term team health and capability building? → Probe: Give me an example where these were in tension.

**Evidence to capture:** Strategic decisions made, trade-offs articulated, business outcomes driven, time horizon of thinking.

#### 3. Organizational Influence

**Definition:** Ability to drive change and alignment beyond their direct reports — influencing peers, stakeholders, and senior leadership.

| Rating | Behavioral Indicators |
|--------|----------------------|
| 5 -- Strong Yes | Has driven cross-organizational initiatives. Influences through data, relationships, and alignment to shared goals. Navigates organizational politics constructively. Builds coalitions. Can tell you who they've influenced and how. |
| 4 -- Yes | Has led initiatives requiring cross-team alignment. Influences peers through reasoning and relationship. Navigates organizational dynamics effectively. Builds support for their team's priorities. |
| 3 -- Mixed | Some evidence of cross-team influence. Can work with peers to align on priorities. Navigates stakeholder relationships reasonably. |
| 2 -- No | Influence is limited to their direct team. Escalates rather than influences. Uncomfortable with organizational dynamics. Relies on authority rather than persuasion. |
| 1 -- Strong No | No evidence of influence beyond direct reports. Adversarial relationship with peers. Cannot navigate organizational complexity. |

**Questions:**

1. Tell me about a time you drove a significant change that required buy-in from people outside your team. → Probe: Who resisted and why? How did you bring them along?
2. Describe your relationship with your peer leaders. How do you resolve disagreements? → Probe: Give me a specific example.

**Evidence to capture:** Cross-org initiatives driven, stakeholder management approach, conflict resolution with peers, influence tactics used.

#### 4. Decision-Making Under Pressure

**Definition:** Quality and speed of decisions in high-stakes, ambiguous, or time-pressured situations.

| Rating | Behavioral Indicators |
|--------|----------------------|
| 5 -- Strong Yes | Makes decisions decisively with incomplete information. Can explain their decision framework. Takes responsibility for outcomes. Knows when to decide fast vs slow. Communicates decisions clearly with rationale. Reverses decisions when evidence demands it. |
| 4 -- Yes | Makes timely decisions with the information available. Explains reasoning clearly. Takes responsibility for results. Communicates decisions and rationale to the team. Willing to revisit decisions when new information emerges. |
| 3 -- Mixed | Makes reasonable decisions under pressure. Can explain their reasoning. Takes responsibility. Communicates decisions. |
| 2 -- No | Analysis paralysis under pressure. Defers decisions upward. Inconsistent decision quality. Poor communication of decisions and rationale. |
| 1 -- Strong No | Cannot make decisions under pressure. Consistently poor judgment. Blames others for outcomes. Hides from tough calls. |

**Questions:**

1. Tell me about the hardest decision you've made as a leader. → Probe: How much time did you have? What information was missing?
2. Describe a time you had to reverse a decision you'd already communicated. → Probe: How did you handle the credibility impact?

**Evidence to capture:** Decision quality, speed, communication, accountability, willingness to reverse when wrong.

#### 5. Team Building & Culture

**Definition:** Creating a team environment where people do their best work — psychological safety, high standards, and clear expectations.

| Rating | Behavioral Indicators |
|--------|----------------------|
| 5 -- Strong Yes | Can describe the culture they've built with specific examples. Balances psychological safety with high standards (both, not either/or). Creates clarity about expectations and how work gets done. Team members contribute diverse perspectives. Addresses culture problems directly. |
| 4 -- Yes | Has intentionally shaped team culture with specific practices. Maintains both safety and standards. Sets clear expectations. Addresses team dynamics issues when they arise. Team engagement is strong. |
| 3 -- Mixed | Has thought about team culture. Creates a reasonable working environment. Sets expectations. Team members seem engaged. |
| 2 -- No | Culture is accidental, not intentional. Either all safety/no standards or all standards/no safety. Unclear expectations. Doesn't address team dynamics issues. |
| 1 -- Strong No | Toxic culture indicators (fear, blame, favoritism). No awareness of their impact on culture. High turnover without self-reflection. |

**Questions:**

1. How would your team describe the culture you've built? What would they say you prioritize? → Probe: What's one thing they'd want you to change?
2. Tell me about a time you had to address a culture or team dynamics problem. → Probe: How did you know it was a problem? What specifically did you do?

**Evidence to capture:** Culture description, specific practices, how they handle tensions (safety vs standards), self-awareness about impact.
