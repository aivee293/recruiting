# Inclusive Language Reference

Comprehensive checklist for detecting and fixing biased language in job descriptions. Organized by bias type with problematic phrasing and neutral alternatives.

---

## Gendered Language

| Problematic | Alternative |
|------------|-------------|
| he/she, his/her | they/their |
| manpower | team, workforce, staffing |
| chairman | chair, chairperson |
| manmade | synthetic, artificial, human-made |
| man-hours | person-hours, work hours |
| guys (addressing team) | team, folks, everyone |
| salesman/saleswoman | salesperson, sales representative |
| foreman | supervisor, lead |
| businessman | business professional, entrepreneur |
| hacker (gendered connotation) | engineer, developer |
| aggressive (in leadership context) | assertive, driven, ambitious |
| nurturing (for leadership roles) | supportive, mentoring, coaching |

**Watch for:** Gendered pronouns in hypothetical scenarios ("the ideal candidate... he will..."). Use "you" or "they" throughout.

## Age-Coded Terms

### Skewing Young

| Problematic | Alternative |
|------------|-------------|
| digital native | comfortable with technology, technically fluent |
| energetic | motivated, driven |
| young team / young company | growing team, early-stage company |
| recent graduate | entry-level, early-career |
| up-and-coming | emerging talent, high-potential |
| fresh perspective | new perspective, different approach |
| startup culture (as youth signal) | describe the actual culture |
| beer fridge / ping pong table | name the actual benefits |

### Skewing Older

| Problematic | Alternative |
|------------|-------------|
| seasoned professional | experienced professional |
| veteran | experienced, accomplished |
| mature | experienced, established |
| traditional background | conventional background |
| proven track record (with excessive years) | demonstrated ability to [specific outcome] |

**Watch for:** "X+ years of experience" when the years don't map to actual skill progression. Ask: does someone with 5 years really have meaningfully different capability than someone with 3?

## Ability-Coded Terms

| Problematic | Alternative |
|------------|-------------|
| must be able to stand for long periods | (remove if desk-based role) |
| strong physical presence | professional presence |
| able-bodied | (remove unless genuinely required, with explanation) |
| walks/runs between locations | travels between locations |
| see/observe (for review tasks) | review, assess, evaluate |
| hear/listen (for communication) | receive, understand, process |
| normal working hours | standard working hours |
| clean-cut appearance | professional appearance |

**Watch for:** Physical requirements listed by default from template copy-paste. Only include physical requirements that are genuinely essential to the role, and explain why.

## Culture-Coded Terms

| Problematic | Why It's Exclusionary | Alternative |
|------------|----------------------|-------------|
| ninja | Trivializes culture, signals bro culture | specialist, expert |
| rockstar | Implies individual heroics over collaboration | high-impact contributor |
| guru | Cultural/religious appropriation | expert, authority, specialist |
| wizard | Gatekeeping connotation | skilled, proficient |
| work hard play hard | Signals long hours + mandatory socializing | describe actual work-life expectations |
| culture fit | Masks bias toward homogeneity | values alignment, working style match |
| like-minded | Signals conformity | aligned on [specific value] |
| tribe | Cultural appropriation | team, group, community |
| crush it | Aggressive language, signals burnout culture | deliver results, excel |
| hustle | Signals overwork expectation | drive, initiative |
| drinking / happy hours | Excludes non-drinkers, some religions | team events, social gatherings |
| family / we're a family | Blurs professional boundaries | supportive team, collaborative environment |
| native English speaker | Discriminatory (and often illegal) | fluent in English, professional English proficiency |

## Requirement Inflation

Requirement inflation is the most common form of exclusion in JDs. It disproportionately deters women, underrepresented minorities, and career changers (who tend to only apply when they meet 100% of requirements vs. 60% for others).

### Degree Requirements

| Problematic | Alternative |
|------------|-------------|
| "BS in Computer Science required" | "BS in CS or equivalent practical experience" |
| "Advanced degree preferred" | Only include if the role genuinely requires graduate-level academic knowledge (research roles, certain data science roles) |
| "Top-tier university" | Remove entirely — signals elitism, not quality |
| "MBA required" | "MBA or equivalent business/operational experience" |

**Decision test:** Would you interview a candidate who has 8 years of exceptional work experience but no degree? If yes, the degree isn't required.

### Years of Experience

| Problematic | Why | Alternative |
|------------|-----|-------------|
| "10+ years of experience" for mid-level role | Inflated to signal seniority; gates out fast learners | "Have led [specific type of project] from design through production" |
| "5 years of Kubernetes" | Technology is ~10 years old; 5 years of production K8s is rare | "Deep Kubernetes production experience" |
| "15+ years in software engineering" for Staff role | Some engineers reach Staff in 7-8 years | "Demonstrated ability to lead complex cross-team technical initiatives" |

**Decision test:** If someone has 3 fewer years than stated but has the outcomes you need, would you interview them? If yes, reframe around outcomes.

### Tool-Specific Requirements

| Problematic | Alternative |
|------------|-------------|
| "Must know Terraform" | "Infrastructure as code experience (Terraform preferred)" |
| "Expert in React and Redux" | "Strong frontend skills in a modern component framework" |
| "Proficient in Salesforce" | "CRM platform experience (Salesforce a plus)" |
| "Must know our tech stack: Go, gRPC, PostgreSQL, Kubernetes" | List as the current stack, frame as "you'll work with", not "you must know" |

**Decision test:** Is it the tool or the underlying skill? Would you hire someone who knows an equivalent tool and can ramp in 2 weeks?

## Exclusionary Framing

### Absolute vs. Flexible Language

| Exclusionary | Inclusive |
|-------------|----------|
| must be | ideally has, we're looking for |
| required | important, valued |
| will not be considered without | strongly preferred |
| non-negotiable | essential for this role because [reason] |
| only candidates with X | candidates who bring X will thrive because... |

### Must-Have Count Check

Research shows women apply when they meet 100% of qualifications; men apply at ~60%.

- **0-3 must-haves:** Appropriate for most roles
- **4-5 must-haves:** Acceptable for senior/specialized roles
- **6+ must-haves:** Almost certainly inflated — audit each one
- **8+ must-haves:** This is a wish list, not a job requirement

**For each must-have, ask:** "If a candidate met every other requirement but lacked this one, would I still interview them?" If yes, move it to nice-to-have.

## Quick Scan Process

When reviewing a JD, scan in this order:

1. **Ctrl+F scan** — search for: he, she, his, her, man, guy, ninja, rockstar, guru, native, young, energetic, aggressive, must, required
2. **Requirements count** — count must-haves. More than 5? Audit each.
3. **Years check** — any years-of-experience that exceed what's genuinely needed?
4. **Degree check** — is the degree truly required, or would equivalent experience work?
5. **Tool check** — are you requiring specific tools when the underlying skill is what matters?
6. **Physical requirements** — any that aren't genuinely essential to the role?
7. **Culture language** — any "culture fit", "family", "hustle" language?
8. **Read aloud test** — would a candidate from a different background, gender, age, or ability feel welcomed by this language?
